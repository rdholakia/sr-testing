### Opening

That instinct — save it somewhere it can't tempt you, let it quietly grow — is exactly right for a goal like this.

Here's the good news: you don't need much. At $100/month for 6 years, you'd land at $8,916. The lower end of your $6K–$10K target only needs $67.29/month to hit at a 7% blended return — so even a modest consistent contribution gets you there.

The 7% figure is illustrative — a blended assumption across HYSA and CD laddering. Real HYSA rates today are in the 4–5% range; even at 4.5%, $100/month over 6 years grows to roughly $8,100. Either way, you get there.

---

### The Tradeoff *(section banner, teal→blue gradient)*

A HYSA is fully liquid (good if plans shift) but rates float. A short-term CD ladder locks in a rate for 12–18 months at a time and typically earns slightly more — the tradeoff is you can't touch it mid-term without a small penalty. For a 6-year goal you don't need to touch, a CD ladder is worth considering once you've built a few months of contributions.

---

### What To Do *(section banner, orange gradient)*

**Open a separate HYSA — this week (~10 min).** Ally Bank (ally.com/bank/online-savings-account) and Marcus by Goldman Sachs (marcus.com/us/en/savings) are solid picks with no minimums and no monthly fees. Name the account "Son's Car Fund" — most HYSAs let you label accounts, and that label does psychological work. Verify the current APY at each site before opening; rates update frequently.

**Set the auto-transfer the same day you open it.** Link it to your checking account and schedule a recurring transfer for the day after your paycheck hits — $100 to start, bump to $150 when it feels comfortable. The goal is to make the decision once and never think about it again. *Watch-for:* set the transfer to pull from checking, not push from savings — "pull" setups are harder to accidentally cancel.

**Consider a CD ladder after month 3 (~20 min, once you have $300–$400 saved).** Once you have a small base, roll a portion into a 12-month CD at your bank or at Discover Bank (discover.com/online-banking/cd). When it matures, roll it again. This locks in a slightly higher rate without touching the principal. *Watch-for:* check the early-withdrawal penalty before opening — most CDs charge 3–6 months of interest, which is small but worth knowing.

**Leave it alone.** Seriously — don't check the balance more than quarterly. The "invisible" feeling you want is real and it works. The account doing its job quietly is the whole point.

---

### What Would Change This *(section banner, purple gradient)*

- If the timeline is closer to 3 years (not 6), the monthly contribution needed roughly doubles — worth revisiting if the child is on the older end of the 9–12 range.
- If you find a CD rate meaningfully above 5%, locking in a 2-year CD now beats a floating HYSA for this goal.
- If high-interest debt (above 6% APR) exists, that gets paid first — the guaranteed return beats the savings rate.
- If the target shifts above $10K, bump the monthly contribution to $150 from the start rather than waiting.

**Your move this week:** open the Ally or Marcus account, name it, and set the auto-transfer. The rest takes care of itself.
