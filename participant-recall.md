# Round 2 — Participant Recall Sheet

Five returning participants from the Aug 5–7 Ask sessions run by Amanda. Each entry covers who they are, the question they actually brought, where their session ended, what landed, what frustrated them, playback lines for the recall opening, and what their session is best used to test.

All quotes are verbatim from the transcripts. Timestamps are session-clock.

## Luke R — Portland, OR · 38 · controller, multi-site mental health practice
**Session:** Aug 6, 56 min

**His question.** Whether and when to move up from the starter house he bought in 2018. He and his wife (works in finance, no kids, three dogs and chickens) want an acre or so outside Portland city limits, partly to escape the additional ~3% city tax on households over $200K. Saving mode. Wants to fund travel — his wife has never been out of the country.

**Where he sits.** Heavy ChatGPT Plus user, two years, web only. Has a financial-advisor friend who advises informally. Validates AI output against his brokerage and his own research.

**How far he got.** Furthest of the five into the options layer. He reached the timeline options (6–9 months / 18–24 / 36+), read the pros and cons, understood "lock it in" unprompted, and chose one. The prototype then broke — the model stopped while building the plan — so he never saw the post-lock-in plan.

**What landed.**
- The reframe. He singled out the "sub-3% mortgage decision" as the moment the product beat ChatGPT: <q>Chat GPT never even addressed that... it just feels like a more human approach.</q>
- Breadth as trust. <q>I love how it's multifaceted. It's not just about the house but bringing in the whole life.</q>
- Time estimates on tasks: <q>if you're feeling lazy on a Saturday... I think if they didn't give that time estimate you started diving in and you got frustrated after an hour and scrapped the whole project.</q>
- Warmth as a differentiator: <q>It doesn't come off as warm [ChatGPT], especially when discussing more difficult financial topics.</q>

**What didn't.**
- Value statement fell flat: <q>it feels like the same pitch that I've seen from a lot of companies... what actually differentiates the product?</q>
- Fiduciary insight tag: <q>I could go without it.</q> Reads as common sense to him; he grants it might help first-time buyers.
- Scrolling was hard for him more than once.

**Playback lines for the opening.** The sub-3%-mortgage moment; "comprehensive, warm, forward/future-facing"; "a robot financial advisor."

**Best used to test.** Reframe-first vs. math-first — he is the strongest advocate for reframe-first, so if pacing the reframe into its own step *weakens* it for him, that's a real signal. Also the only participant who can compare a stepped analysis against a remembered unspooled one at the options layer.

## Heather C — metro Detroit, MI · 37 · customer service (home improvement) + home bakery
**Session:** Aug 5, 69 min

**Her question.** Saving for her son Nolan's first car — a few years of runway, wants something safe, not much to set aside, and wants the money to <q>feel like it doesn't exist.</q> Three kids (13, 10, 5), two autistic; husband is a plant operator. Debt-payoff goal running alongside.

**Where she sits.** Microsoft Copilot daily for eight months — finances, medical (translates lab results and clinician notes both directions), trip planning. Rocket Money for goal tracking. Describes herself as neurodivergent and says finance sites overstimulate her. Screenshots advice and sets Alexa reminders because she'll otherwise forget.

**How far she got.** Furthest overall. Went through gathering, analysis, options — she reached for compare **unprompted** — locked in, and got all the way into the generated plan with the 8-step / "this week 0 of 4" progress structure.

**What landed.**
- Chunking and progress. <q>I love this right here. It says 0 out of 8 steps. I love a visual showing progress.</q> And on the weekly split: <q>I like this condensing it because 8 steps does feel like quite a bit.</q>
- Visuals over prose. <q>This is what I kind of need to see. I kind of need to see like a visual... instead of a whole paragraph, I kind of get lost in the paragraphs.</q>
- Time estimates and specificity: <q>it doesn't have a time stamp and this really kind of... tells me the exact steps so I know what to expect. There's no surprise.</q>
- Emotional state at the plan: <q>I feel committed to it. I actually feel a little excited... it feels like I'm being guided. My hand's being held through this.</q>
- Sourcing. Liked seeing NerdWallet cited; wanted a date stamp on it.

**What she asked for that doesn't exist.**
- Push it further: <q>make it to where I really don't have to do much... I don't want it to be a little more pushy</q> [sic — she means she *does* want it pushier, in a nice way]. Wants it to walk her through the actual bank UI, and to offer to automate the transfer.
- Decision support at the commitment step: on "you have a single dollar amount written down," she said <q>I would ask it to help me with that because I struggle with the decision making. Once I'm committed, I'm committed.</q>
- Saved progress, multiple concurrent plans that account for each other, clickable links, push notifications, Face ID.

**What didn't.**
- "Expert moves": <q>I kind of take that like somebody who is not in my shoes, who I don't know. So I'm not 100 on that one.</q>
- Ranges as inputs — she struggles to choose between two brackets and defaults upward to be safe.

**Playback lines.** The 0-of-8 progress moment; "my hand's being held through this"; "I get lost in the paragraphs."

**Best used to test.** Pacing directly — she is the clearest voice for stepping and progress structure, and the most likely to tell you if the new check-in beat reads as guidance or as friction. Also the best read on whether the interactive modules replace prose successfully.

## Whitney E — Forney, TX (outside Dallas) · 40 · sales strategy consultant, healthcare
**Session:** Aug 5, 65 min

**Her question.** Financing a ~$45,000 truck. Real and current — daycare costs ending as her son starts kindergarten frees up money. Two kids (5 and 1), husband is a personal trainer with a TV-mounting business. Both have 401(k)s and a pension. Uses Chase's goal-setter for trip and vehicle savings.

**Where she sits.** ChatGPT Plus, treats it as <q>a guide... I don't use it as a, the Bible.</q> Frames her motivation as generational wealth and as a generational break from her mother's save-everything-no-fun posture.

**How far she got.** Through gathering and analysis into the three-way lender comparison (dealer / credit union / online lender), plus a follow-up on qualification difficulty. Did not lock in.

**The central finding — she wanted the math first and didn't get it.** She said versions of this at least four times:
- <q>I was hoping it would give me kind of like it did here... I'm just curious why it didn't give me the math or the full on math.</q>
- <q>Still good information. But I don't know what my monthly payment is gonna look like. What's my interest rate?</q>
- <q>I just wish they would give me like this information here. What would change this? I would like to see that like the math.</q>
- On finally reaching it: <q>this is my expectation. This is what I was thinking it was going to give me before the numbers... What could change this? That's fine. I think that information is helpful, but I want to get the numbers. That's what I'm looking for.</q>

**What landed.**
- Options as leverage. <q>it gives me leverage... I'm gonna have something in my back pocket versus just walking in and just basically taking their word for it.</q> The online lender was net-new to her: <q>online lender was not even something that I even thought about.</q>

**What didn't.**
- Trade-off text she couldn't act on: <q>Maximum leverage at dealership you control — yeah that's not helpful.</q>
- The save-version copy: <q>That's a lot of words.</q> She got there, but only after re-reading.
- Wanted notification on rate change; expected the tool to move to "find me the inventory" next.

**Playback lines.** "I want to get the numbers"; the ChatGPT-vs-schooling line; "it gives me leverage."

**Best used to test.** Math-first vs. reframe-first — she is the clean counterweight to Luke. If the new paced version still buries her payment figure, she'll say so immediately and the ordering question is settled.

Debt-free apart from house and cars; house bought well below means; still paying his wife's student loans. Under $25K saved across retirement accounts, no supplemental accounts yet. **Where he sits.** ChatGPT Plus, heavy — home repairs, gym routines, cooking. Values that it has <q>learned me</q>, and flagged upfront that this gave ChatGPT an unfair advantage in the comparison; he volunteered to augment the Hence prompt to level it.

**How far he got.** Through gathering and analysis into the options panel; picked an option; did not complete lock-in. Hit a data snag where a housing/take-home figure was misread, which he caught himself.

**What landed.**

## Chad S — Kansas City area, KS · 45 · owns a restaurant/brewery and a new production brewery
**Session:** Aug 7, 66 min

**His question.** Getting household debt under control after a costly year, and building a budget. Married (wife is an elementary principal and a spender), two girls, 4 and 6. Commutes over an hour to the businesses. Household take-home ~$11K/mo. Says the thing on his mind is <q>stress and anxiety</q> and that he's constantly thinking about money for three separate entities.

**Where he sits.** No advisor. ChatGPT for practical wins — he consolidated streaming services and got Peacock free through Walmart+. Bank spending tool for one-time snapshots, not tracking. Self-describes as having <q>pretty bold ADHD</q> and jumping between conversations. Says his magic-wand ask is advice that's <q>correct, timely, and I would not have to research them at all.</q>

**How far he got.** Through gathering and analysis, into the options and expert-moves layer, and engaged closely with the confidence meter. Did not lock in — <q>lots of information gathering before locking it in.</q> He gave estimated rather than real numbers (didn't know his APRs off the top of his head) and hit a take-home discrepancy the system surfaced.

**What landed — the strongest existing evidence for spatial chunking.**
- <q>the bold colors, the separate cards make it very clear that there's differentiation. And this makes learning about these things seem a little less overwhelming because if this was all... this starts to get really long. And when things get long, you know, you start skimming or scanning.</q>
- <q>it lets you kind of decide how you want to... how you function.</q> High level on the left, cards on the right to go deeper: <q>this is very well put together because it does give me the high level and lets me continue to gain knowledge.</q>
- Tone. He liked emotion-neutral flatly — <q>it's being very emotion neutral</q>, <q>some other AI platforms can be a little too yes-man, rah-rah</q> — which is the opposite of Luke's warmth preference.
- Accountability as trust: the system telling him it can't give a real plan without real numbers. <q>Accuracy is one of the biggest challenges to me overall with AI. And even just something as simple as providing its own oversight helps me feel a little more confident.</q>
- The confidence meter: <q>even though it's saying that the confidence is moderate here and only 55%, it actually instills a much higher percentage of trust.</q>

**What he asked for that doesn't exist.**
- A one-line explanation of the confidence meter, for a specific reason: <q>it might change the way someone would react to an inconsistency.</q> He wasn't sure whether it was cross-referencing everything, and that uncertainty affected whether he'd stop and correct his income figure.

**What didn't.**
- Expert-moves language: <q>it seems like it's trying to back up the idea that this is an expert move with language that's a little bit higher level. The average person might think it seems a little too much or over describing.</q>
- Data entry felt <q>easy, yet a little tedious</q> — about a half hour of lookup, which he accepted because he understood why.
- New-service discomfort: <q>it almost does feel a little invasive just because it's new.</q>

**Playback lines.** "When things get long you start skimming"; the 55%-confidence-raises-trust line; "correct, timely, and I would not have to research them."

**Best used to test.** Whether pacing over *time* (clicking forward through steps) delivers what he got from chunking in *space* (panels and cards) — he articulated the spatial version so clearly that he's the one most likely to notice if the temporal version is worse. Also the confidence label.

## Cross-cutting notes for the guide

**Compare was used.** Heather reached for it unprompted and named it as enticing; Luke reached the options layer and engaged. Whitney's whole session was effectively a comparison exercise. The read that "nobody used compare" is a time artifact — most sessions spent 20+ minutes on ChatGPT warm-up and value-statement reactions before reaching the product.

**Two tone constituencies, opposed.** Luke and Heather want warmth and want it pushier. Chad wants emotion-neutral and reacted against rah-rah tone. Josh sits in between and prizes factual restraint. Don't design a tone test into this round expecting consensus.

**Time estimates split.** Luke and Heather cited them as the best small feature in the product. Josh distrusted them as unverifiable. If they survive, they need to be honest ranges or provenance-marked.

**Three of five went looking for the confidence signal unprompted, and all three wanted it explained.** That is the cheapest closable decision available in this round.

**Nobody has seen a real handoff.** Luke's lock-in broke; Josh and Chad chose not to lock in; Whitney didn't reach it. Only Heather saw a generated plan. The plan/priority layer is effectively untested.

**Data entry is a real cost and they know it.** Chad estimated a half hour and didn't know his APRs. Josh knew his numbers to the cent but had to be re-asked. Heather struggles with ranges. Whitney wanted to play with the numbers herself rather than answer questions about them.
